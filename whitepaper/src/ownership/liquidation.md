# 2D. Liquidation

To ensure the proper functioning of the system, borrowers must adhere to the terms of the agreement and repay their debt within the agreed timeframe.

Borrowers who have an unpaid outstanding balance and a loan that has expired, reaching the specified loan-to-value (LTV) threshold, are subject to liquidation by collateral liquidators. This means that the borrower's entire debt will be due.

If a borrower is close to exceeding the agreed threshold, they will have a maximum of 12 hours to repay the loan. If the debt remains unpaid after this period, the collateral liquidators may pay off the borrower's loan before it reaches the threshold of 0.0133%. In the event of liquidation, the collateral liquidators will receive a reward of up to 51%.

Liquidations are carried out by third-party entities.

When a loan is liquidated, it has negative consequences for the borrower's credit score, making it more challenging to obtain credit in the future. Additionally, the following penalty measures will be imposed:

The borrower's credit reputation will be negatively affected.
The borrower will not be eligible for future loans with a credit limit higher than $200.
Borrowers with outstanding balances will be subject to a higher interest rate of 21% on repayments.
If a borrower intends to default on their loan, it will be liquidated.
